What it is for

A full-bleed brand moment: a closing band, a cover, a card in a deck, a social image. Something that is about Peace Intel rather than something a person operates.

What it is not for

Text of any size, a control of any kind, or a fill behind content. #FFFFFF on the light end measures under 3:1, and it carries no state, so a person cannot learn what it means by using it.

Between 2003 and 2011 the conflict in Darfur drew three distinct intervention efforts, and they are worth separating because they failed for different reasons.

The African Union deployed AMIS in 2004 with a monitoring mandate it was never resourced to fulfil ●1. The figure was measured, it did not accumulate. A damped rise says that; a spinning counter says the opposite. The travel doubled to 16px and the duration to 1100ms, for one reason: a settle is only legible if you can watch it decelerate, and eight pixels in half a second has no deceleration in it the eye can find.

.mo-dealon a citation list · order carries meaning

Ordered by tier, so the sequence itself says which evidence is strongest. This is the only place order is allowed to mean something; everywhere else a stagger is just delay.

How the group reveal was decidedfive candidates · one kept

Five ways of bringing a group of things onto the page were built and compared on the two cases that matter, a card and a citation list. .mo-unmask treated the block as one object and revealed it as a surface, which said nothing about the order inside it. .mo-stagger carried the order but faded each item, and a fade at 10px mono is a flicker. The three syntheses asked whether the sequence could be carried by the mask instead of by opacity: .mo-cascade timed the children to one travelling edge, .mo-uncover gave every child its own mask with no opacity anywhere, and .mo-deal added a six-pixel rise and a tighter edge to that. Deal is the one kept, on 2026-09-26. The other four are removed from the stylesheet rather than left in as options, because a motion set with five ways to do one thing has no grammar.

What to look for. On the card, whether the reveal reads as one thing arriving or as three things arriving separately — a card is one thing, so the second is wrong however pleasant it looks. On the list, whether you can still tell which source came first, because that order is the epistemics and the only reason to animate a citation list at all.

The cost of the choice, stated. Deal is the most animated of the five and the only one that moves, so it is the one most likely to read as a template on second viewing. The mask does most of the work and the rise is six pixels, which is what keeps it the short side of that line. Use it where the arrival is the point, and not on something a reader will pass four times in a session.

The one property lost with .mo-uncover is worth naming: it changed no opacity anywhere, which made it safe on 9 and 10px mono at any count. Deal moves the item instead of fading it, so it keeps most of that — type is at full density the instant it is visible, it is just arriving from six pixels below.

Cut 15 October: resolve, develop, focus and rule. Matt kept .mo-deal and retired the rest, so the system has one entry move. The pages that used them now deal their groups instead. This is the plate as it stood.

Everything here is between two and three times slower than it was. The old set ran 320 to 520ms on the reasoning that interface motion should be quick, which is right for a control answering a click and wrong for a reveal: a reveal that finishes before the eye arrives at it did not happen. Resolve was the signature move of the system and it was the one nobody could see. Entry motion now runs 900 to 1600ms. Hover, focus and press are untouched. The test is whether you can describe what happened after seeing it once; if the answer is something appeared, it is too fast.

The original plate introduction, the four mounts, the corners-versus-registration comparison and registration itself follow as they were.

Framing the one thing that matters.

Registration was the only framing device the system had, which made it the answer to every question, which is how a signature turns into a tic. It is also the loudest thing here: four accent brackets converging is an event, and most figures want to be presented rather than announced. Under 4px corners it reads angular besides — the brackets are the last right angle left on the page.

So it becomes one of eight and stops being the default. Each of these marks a block as the figure, and each says something slightly different about it. None of them uses chroma unless it is opted into.

One per view, and preferably one per page. The rule that governed registration governs all of them, for the same reason: a page where three things are framed has nothing framed. Mixing two different mounts on one page is worse than using the same one twice, because then the difference between them has to mean something, and it does not.

Finding

Two corners, on a diagonal.

Registration’s idea at a whisper: a hairline elbow inside the block rather than outside it, with the elbow rounded to the same 4px as everything else. Four corners is a frame, and a frame that does not close reads as one that failed to load. Two lets the eye complete the rectangle, which is more elegant than being handed it.

AR 11Gradient fieldsFrom Pl. 12 · Cut 15 Oct 2026

The history of the fields, cut so the plate states what a field is rather than how it got there. The live contrast readouts that sat under two of these bands were retired with it.

An atmospheric background built only from tokens. Three rules hold it together. Monochrome: paper and ink, no accent anywhere in the ramp. Asymmetrical: no blob is centred, no two are the same size, and two of the three are anchored off-canvas, so the field reads as something much larger that the viewport happens to be cropping. A centred radial is a spotlight. Grainy: ordered Bayer dithering against five stops, at the same cell as the dove and the magnifying glass.

The ramp spans the page tone to --line-2, fourteen percent of ink across five stops. It went to --wash-1 first, at three percent, and three percent turned out to be past quiet into invisible: on a real page the field was a rumour. Fourteen is the hairline tone, so the darkest part of a field is exactly as dark as the line around a card. The field never introduces a value the page does not already use. The step before that was fifteen percent of the warm tone, which is a different failure: that one carried chroma and read as a thing on the page.

And every field moves now. The opt-in inverted: a field is live unless it carries .gfield--still. The two changes are one decision. A still field at this weight is not atmosphere, it is a slightly uneven sheet of paper — the movement is what makes that weight legible as weather rather than as a printing fault. And the ramp got quiet enough that it safely can move, which is what retired the old restriction to dawn.

The chroma came out on 25 Sep, and it was the right call twice over. The old ramp mixed ink into the accent on the argument that mud stops a large orange wash reading as a sale banner. True, and solving the wrong problem: a field is the largest object on whatever page it appears on, so whatever colour it carries becomes the page’s colour, and an atmospheric wash of brand orange in front of an institutional reader is a consumer move however muddy it is. It also forced the text-safety exception below, because a ramp with hue in it reaches a value dark enough that --ink-70 stops clearing AA over the bottom stop.

With no hue there is nothing for a headline to fight, nothing to clash with a photograph laid over it, and nothing to re-tune if the accent ever changes. The chroma-free variant built last round was the test of this, and it read better than any of the warm ones, so it stopped being a variant. Muddy now means tonal: three ellipses of the same grey family compounding into values none of them contains on its own.

The cell is 3, and it is measured rather than chosen. The question was whether the system matched the original site, and it did not: run lengths in hero-dove.mp4 and hero-glass.mp4 give a modal cell of two source pixels in a 720px master drawn across a 1120px stage, which is 3.1 CSS pixels on screen. The handoff clips were at 1.6 and the fields at 2, so the brand’s own assets had always been about twice as coarse as anything built since. Three is what makes it read as distinctly pixelated rather than merely fine-grained, and one number now governs the fields, the clips, the rendered grid and the motion primitives.

The mapping is the same idea as the gradient fields, applied to footage: stop treating the asset as an image with colours of its own, and read it as luminance that the palette then answers. Seven stops, ordered by luminance rather than by index, so the same ramp is correct in both themes without a second declaration. A bright sky stays bright on a dark page.

The first runtime version was worse than the baked one, and the reason is worth keeping. It mapped the mono master, which has only seven input levels, so nearest-level rounding collapsed the sun in the war clip into the sky. The baked version was mapping the original footage, with its full tonal range, and it kept the highlight. Comparing them was comparing a seven-level source to a continuous one, not two methods.

So the master is now plain greyscale, undithered, and the dither happens at runtime: luminance is scaled across the ramp and an 8×8 Bayer threshold decides which of the two neighbouring stops each pixel takes. That is the same algorithm as the gradient fields, which is why they belong in the same system. The sun is back.

The runtime version is now the one to argue for. It is the smallest file of the three, and the only version that cannot go out of sync with the palette. Bake the colour in and every future change to the accent means re-exporting every clip; map at runtime and the clips follow the tokens the way everything else does.

Switching to dark exposed a second problem, and it is the more interesting one. Mapping luminance faithfully means a bright scene stays bright, which on a dark page turns the clip into a lit slab. That is worse than the grey rectangle this section set out to fix. The fix depends on what the footage is, so the mapping takes a mode. A subject on a seamless reverses the ramp on dark, so the white ground becomes the page and the subject lifts off it, which is the same move as screen-instead-of-multiply done in the palette. A scene compresses the ramp instead and never reaches the paper tone, because reversing it would make the sky dark and the ground bright. Neither is a preference; pick by what the footage is.

What it costs, measured. Stepping the clip count up on this page: nought, one and two clips all hold 121fps; three drops it to 31. The runtime mapping is not the cost — it measures 1.3ms per frame at twelve frames a second, about one and a half percent of one core, and switching it off while three clips play changes nothing. Nor is it decode: setting those same three to visibility: hidden while they keep playing returns the page to 121fps. It is compositing the upscaled frames. These are small clips blown up with image-rendering: pixelated, and past two on screen the paint cost falls off a cliff.

So the answer to whether this slows a site down is no, with one condition attached. One clip per page, which is already the tier-one rule in Pl. 09, measured 121fps — indistinguishable from a page with nothing on it. What is expensive is a viewport holding several treated clips at once, and this plate is the only place that happens. It caps itself at two playing for that reason. If a page ever wants two moving clips in one view, measure it before shipping it; everything else here is free.

With script off the greyscale master is what shows. Undithered greyscale is a plainer fallback than the mono clip was, so if a page cannot rely on script, ship the mono file there instead and accept the second asset.

The war footage is a stock generation, not a licensed asset, and it is here to test the treatment rather than to propose the shot. Worth saying before anyone falls in love with it. The point it proves is that a scene can join the brand without being shot against a seamless, which widens what the library can contain considerably.

.veil

Atmosphere

A subject on a seamless, where the ground blends away entirely and a dithered silhouette floats on the paper with no box and no edge. .vmap

A figure

Footage that fills its frame has no ground to lose, so it has to be presented as what it is: opaque, mapped through the ramp, looked at rather than through.
