What happened in Darfur between 2003 and 2011? Who intervened, what did they do, and what resulted?
Peace Intel

Three interventions, three different failures.

Three distinct efforts, worth separating because they failed for different reasons. The African Union deployed AMIS in 2004 with a monitoring mandate it was never resourced to fulfil ●1. Its successor UNAMID was the largest peacekeeping operation in the world at its peak, yet its civilian protection record remains contested ◐2.

A frequently cited displacement figure does not trace to a primary source in this corpus ↗3, so treat it with caution.

A3 Tiers · rule-row + evi-badge
How to read a citation

Solid, hollow, dashed.

Not every source is equally strong. The shape carries the meaning, so the tiers survive a greyscale print and a colourblind reader.

● Tier 1 ingested

Full text in the corpus

The citation points at the passage, and you can read the surrounding argument.

◐ Tier 2 abstract

Abstract only

Enough to place a finding, not enough to verify how it was reached.

↗ Tier 3 web

Found outside the corpus

Carries no accent at all, because it has not been through the same scrutiny.

A4 Method · sticky head + .step
What it does with a question

Four moves, all of them visible.

The work a researcher would do, done in the open so you can see where each part of the answer came from.

01

Ask in plain language

A real question about a real place. No search syntax and no keywords to guess.

02

Read the full text

Sources are fully parsed and semantically processed, so retrieval works on what a study says rather than on its abstract.

03

Compare across studies

It follows citation chains, sets findings side by side, and connects the question to the most relevant cases.

04

Cite to the passage

Every claim links to the study behind it, and every citation points to the exact passage.
