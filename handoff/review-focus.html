Do livelihood programmes reduce recruitment?

Do livelihood programmes reduce recruitment into armed groups? Lead with effect sizes.
Peace Intel · 10:13 AM

Not on their own. Across the strongest studies, income alone has a small effect or none on whether young men join armed groups. Programmes do better when they reach the people armed groups are already approaching.

1. Unemployment is a weak predictor of violence

Berman and colleagues found no positive relationship between unemployment and insurgent violence in Afghanistan, Iraq or the Philippines; where there was a relationship it ran the other way . The study is from 2011, older than this project’s 2015 line.

Mercy Corps’ interviews with young people reached a similar conclusion: experiences of injustice, discrimination and corruption explained participation in violence better than joblessness did . UNDP’s interviews with former recruits across the Sahel, a web source outside the library, give a more mixed picture, with employment named more often than in earlier rounds .

2. Where livelihood support has moved behaviour

The strongest test is a randomised trial with high-risk young men in Liberia. Agricultural training and capital shifted men into farming and away from illegal mining, and reduced their interest in mercenary work during the 2011 Ivorian crisis; much of that effect came from the promise of future payments rather than income already earned .

3. What it means for the portfolio

Weight the programmes that target young people already approached by armed groups and that pair income with a stake in local decisions. Treat claims of reduced recruitment as unproven where a programme measures jobs created rather than recruitment itself.
