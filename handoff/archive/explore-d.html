Who it is for

Three desks, one problem.

Different institutions, different documents. The same pressure: a decision is due, and someone will ask where the evidence came from.

Question Philanthropy Foreign service Development bank
You answer to A board and its strategy committee A minister, and in time a parliament A review panel, then a board of executive directors
You write Board papers, portfolio reviews, grant due diligence Submissions, briefing notes, reporting from post Risk and resilience assessments, appraisal annexes, completion reports
The question underneath Is this where our money makes the most difference? What are the options, and what happens if we do nothing? Could this operation do harm, and what did the last one learn?
Where it stalls The literature review is commissioned, and arrives after the decision. The desk officer is new to the country and has a week. Lessons learned are copied forward from the last project.
What you get Where the evidence points, and the gaps worth funding A cited brief with its confidence stated Lessons traced to the sources that recorded them

Continuity

Your predecessor read all of this. You have a week.

Postings rotate

A desk officer inherits a country, and the reading that came with it leaves with the last one.

Programme officers move on

A strategy outlives the people who researched it, and the reasons get lost first.

Task teams change

The team that appraises an operation is rarely the one that completes it.

A thread keeps the question, the sources and the answer together, so the next person starts where you stopped.
