# 00 · Project Brief

## The one-liner

> **The largest corpus of peacebuilding evidence in one place, accessible through a
> custom-built cognitive agent.** — Matt Gillespie, 2026-07-08

Peace Intel is a curated, citable evidence platform for the peacebuilding field. A visitor
asks a real question — *"What actually works to de-escalate this kind of conflict?"*,
*"Is this grant proposal grounded in evidence?"* — and a cognitive agent answers from a
rigorously curated library of research, evaluations, and case data, **with citations that
link back to the source.**

## What it is (and is not)

| It **is** | It is **not** |
|-----------|---------------|
| A cognitive agent grounded in a curated, citable knowledge base | A general-purpose chatbot (ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude clone) |
| A trusted library — "an art gallery," curated and credible | Open-web search |
| A guide *(the agent)* fronting an institution *(the library)* | A flashy, effects-heavy AI demo |
| A decision-support tool for peacemakers and funders | A replacement for expert judgment |

The recurring internal framing: **"a cognitive agent, never a chatbot."** The differentiators
are (1) a curated, citable knowledge base, (2) exclusive practitioner data not on the open
web, and (3) a proprietary graph-database memory architecture ("Graffiti"). See
[`05-content-and-data-model.md`](05-content-and-data-model.md).

## Who it's for

Ordered by strategic priority as of 2026-07-08:

1. **Funders / portfolio managers (PRIMARY).** EPI's bet: funders are the highest-leverage
   node in the ecosystem — they change *what gets done*. Harder to convert, higher impact.
2. **Practitioners / project & program managers.** Easier to convert, lower leverage. The
   people designing interventions, writing theories of change, assessing proposals.
3. **Researchers / scholars.** Lower volume, but essential for *credibility*. The platform
   must be rigorous enough that academics trust it.

A core design tension, stated by Ahn: **credible enough for scholars, accessible enough for
practitioners and funders.** See [`06-personas-and-journeys.md`](06-personas-and-journeys.md).

## Why now / why it matters

- Peacebuilding decisions are made with weak access to evidence. Academic data hubs exist
  but "don't even know how to get started" turning into usable tools.
- EPI occupies a rare niche: **"the space between vibe coding and quality"** — real technical
  execution *plus* deep sector knowledge. No other org was found doing both.
- Organizational stakes are real: EPI has depended heavily on Lauenstein Foundation funding.
  A credible, launchable Peace Intel is central to diversifying funding in Q4 2026 and beyond.

## What "good" looks like at launch

Milt's directive (2026-07-08): when Peace Intel launches, it must be **seen as useful and
valuable** — not trying to do everything, but clearly serving funders and peacemaking
organizations. A poor first reception damages long-term prospects. Design for a *confident,
narrow, credible* first impression over a broad, thin one.

The "North Star" framing (Nathanael, 2026-07-08): imagine what the best-resourced
intelligence agency, think tank, and top academics would produce given unlimited resources to
answer a peacebuilding question — then work backward from that ideal.

## The prototype's job

Give Nathanael a clear, buildable target for the **January launch** MVP, and give Ahn/Milt/
the board something concrete to test and rally around. Two surfaces (see
[`02-information-architecture.md`](02-information-architecture.md)):

- **Marketing site** — the brand narrative, the pitch, the "why," a way in. Public.
- **The application** — the agent + library experience. The actual tool.
