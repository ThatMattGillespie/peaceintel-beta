## Decisions

| Question | Call |
|---|---|
| Audience | Pitch artifact for Ray & Al. Unlisted, `noindex`. |
| Rotary mark | **Rotary's Masterbrand Signature, used as issued.** Full colour. |
| Closing CTA | Claims the product is live: "Start building your application". **Flagged below.** |
| Partnership claim | Capability, not relationship: "built around Rotary International's published criteria". |
| Headline | Option D |
| Subhead | Option C |

**One flag, stated once.** The close claims the product is available today; the private beta opens
January 2027. Fine as a demo of the pitch, needs a different verb before a Rotarian sees it.
Everything else on the page is true today.

**Two things deliberately absent.** The `<3%` grant success rate (unsourced, dropped 2026-09-22)
and any RAGFP endorsement (unconfirmed). Neither returns without provenance.

**The Rotary mark cannot be recoloured.** Rotary's Brand Center does not permit altering the
Masterbrand Signature, so the monochrome treatment originally wanted has no on-guideline version.
It is therefore the one object on the page outside the brand palette, and the only chroma that is
not the accent. The footer uses Rotary's own reversed (white) file rather than an inverted colour
one, because an inverted signature is an altered signature.

## 2 · Written the way Rotary reads

Was three cards and a paragraph, which was three ways of saying one thing. Now one claim beside
its proof: Rotary's own two documents, treated like every other figure in the system.

| Slot | Copy |
|---|---|
| Eyebrow | `WRITTEN THE WAY ROTARY READS` |
| Headline | **We read Rotary's rules so your club doesn't have to.** |
| Body | Rotary publishes exactly what a peace grant has to contain: the project types it will fund, the five elements every application must include, and the measures it accepts. Peace Intel has those criteria built in, next to the peacebuilding evidence base, so your application is checked as you write it. |
| Figure | The Global Grant Application Template and the Peacebuilding and Conflict Prevention Guidelines, dithered through the brand ramp |

The eyebrow was "What this is", which described the section rather than speaking to the reader.
"A better shot at the grant" replaced it and was then judged too transactional (2026-09-28).
"Written the way Rotary reads" names the actual service: an application shaped by the reader
who decides it.

### Positioning lines not taken

| # | Line | Claim strength |
|---|---|---|
| B | developed with Rotary peacebuilding leaders | True (two interviews with Ray), vaguer, implies more. |
| C | EPI and Rotary International have partnered to… | Hold until Rotary signs. |

**Why the field-level stake is not on the page.** The zero-sum argument — Rotary's grant pool is
finite, so better peace applications move more of it to peacebuilding — is the most compelling
reason this collaboration matters, and it is an argument for *Ray and Al*, not for a Rotarian. A
club president reading that they are competing with the water grants is a club president who feels
worse about applying. **Say it on the call, not on the page.**

## 3 · From idea to application

A sticky figure on the left, four steps on the right. The figure swaps as each step reaches the
reading line — 44% of the viewport, the same line the registration motif locks against.

| Slot | Copy |
|---|---|
| Eyebrow | `HOW IT WORKS` |
| Headline | **From idea to application.** |
| Sub | Four steps. Start wherever your club actually is: with a project in mind, or with nothing but the wish to do something useful. |

| # | Step | Body | Figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Pick your intervention area | Tell us the country and what your club cares about. Peace Intel pulls the conflict context and matches it to the project types Rotary will fund under peace and conflict resolution, so you start inside the rules. | a globe |
| 02 | Connect your approach to real evidence | For every part of your project we find what has been tried in comparable places and what happened. We say plainly whether the evidence is strong, mixed or untested, and name the one or two sources that carry the weight. | an open journal, a page lifting off it |
| 03 | Refine with best practices | We check the project against Rotary's own requirements: the community assessment, the training plan, the trainers, the sustainability plan, the monitoring plan. Anything missing gets named, with one line on what would fix it. | a clipboard |
| 04 | Synthesise into a complete application | Every field drafted or annotated in your own voice, with a one-page rationale your club can read and a monitoring plan built on a measure Rotary already recognises. | a finished report, olive branch resting on it |

**Focus is added to the active step, never subtracted from the others.** The usual build dims the
inactive steps; at any opacity below 1 the body copy drops under 4.5:1, so three of the four steps
would be a contrast failure to decorate the one being read. Instead the active step's number takes
the accent and its copy steps up from `--ink-70` to `--ink`. Contrast only ever rises.

**The borehole callout was cut** as too far into the weeds for this page. The finding (Nigeria,
Grady et al., *PNAS* 2023) is still the strongest recruiting argument available — it reframes
peacebuilding as how you design the water project the club was already planning — and is worth
having in your pocket for the conversation.

## Voice rules this copy follows

From `rotary-global-grant-applicant.md`: prescriptive not informational; plain language always (no
"theory of change", no "logframe", no "intervention" as a noun for the project); low citation
density; speak to the club indirectly, because whatever the tool produces has to survive a club
conversation; nothing political; short sentences and no idiom, because an international partner
club may be reading it in translation.
