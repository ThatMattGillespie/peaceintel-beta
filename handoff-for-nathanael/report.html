01Summary

Two programmes rest on strong evidence; two claim more than any study supports.

Of the five livelihood programmes, two rest on strong evidence for their stated aim and one on moderate evidence. Two claim an effect on recruitment that no study in this project supports.

The finding

Income alone rarely moves recruitment into armed groups. Support that reaches the young people armed groups are already approaching has the strongest record.

02The evidence

What the evidence supports

Income alone rarely moves recruitment into armed groups ●1 ◐2. Support that reaches young people already approached by armed groups, and pairs income with a stake in local decisions, has the strongest record ●3.

Conflict-resolution training has the most rigorous recent evidence in the region, from a 2026 cluster trial in Niger ●4.

03The programmes

The five programmes

Programmes A and C target the right people and measure the right outcome. Programme B measures jobs created, and would need a recruitment measure to support its claim. Programmes D and E are cash-for-work schemes with no violence outcome: fund them for income, which they deliver, and not for peace, which they do not measure.

04Gaps

Gaps worth funding

No study in this project follows participants for more than three years, and none covers women recruited into armed groups. Either would be a contribution to the field as well as to the portfolio.
