Three principleswhat every page should look like it follows

The evidence leads

Citations, tier marks and the source line carry the brand. Nothing decorative competes with them, and nothing is said without saying where it came from.

Ink on paper, one accent

Print history, made by a machine

Every colour, size, space, radius and weight is a token. A raw value in a page is off the system.

Fix it at the base

Pl. 01ColourStable · contrast measured live
Ink on paper, and one accent.

Contrast ratios are computed live from the rendered CSS. They are measured against --ground, the tone a band or a tray sits in, rather than the page background, so a token that passes here passes anywhere on the page. The console’s sides sit on --ground-2, the darkest tone text goes on; there --ink-55 is the floor for small text.

Textratios vs the current surface
Surfaces & linesborders stay alpha so they composite on any surface

Two tones besides the page, and a face and a fill made from them. A tone sets back: --ground for a band or the tray’s recess, --ground-2 for the console’s sides and project page. An edge makes an object: --edge (1px of --line-2) round --face, which is the page tone. --fill is the ground’s tone on a small object meant to be quieter than its neighbours, a badge, an avatar, your own message. Which to use where is Pl. 04.

Accent--accent for graphics and large type · --accent-text under 24px

The orange fails AA for body text by a hair. Rather than change hue, the token splits. Production changed hue instead, which is how the live site ended up rendering two brands by route. That brown was rejected on 24 Sep; this is the replacement.

Warm--warm · three uses, and no fourth

The July paper tone. It is a colour, not a surface, and it is documented here rather than in Pl. 04 because that distinction is the whole rule. There was a .surface--warm band in the surfaces plate and it has been removed: a band of warm turns the emphasis tone into a second page tone, which is exactly how the tone stopped meaning anything the first time. Warm earns its place by being rare, and a section background is never rare.

Pl. 02TypeStable · Source Serif 4 · Barlow · Space Mono
A light serif, a workhorse, an instrument.

Source Serif 4 for what is read as a headline, set light: 400 at every size, the two largest included (--serif-w-display was 500 until 16 October, when display grew from 46 to 64px and the size took over the weight’s job). Barlow for everything read at length, at 16px. Space Mono for labels, readings and the eyebrow ladder. Nothing serif is bold, and nothing is set below 10px.

The sans is Barlow (16 October), at the system’s own tracking. DIN 2014 would have needed a paid web licence; Barlow is free under the SIL Open Font License, has a true SemiBold for 600, and covers the same languages as the serif. It was chosen in the sans test over Clear Sans and D-DIN, neither of which has a 600.

The serif is never set in capitals. A label in capitals is the instrument register, so it is set in the mono (.eyebrow): Key assertions, Details, Summary, Sources. Source Serif in capitals reads as a wedding invitation, and its small sizes lose their shape. The console’s serif heading rule leaves any heading marked .eyebrow in the mono, so a label can be a real heading.

Headlines stay to two lines. At 64px a headline of about 32 to 40 characters fits on two lines in the hero’s measure; the studied gallery heroes have a median of five words. A longer thought is a headline plus a lede, not a longer headline. Home’s (69 characters) and Rotary’s (89) run to four lines at the new size and are the first to rewrite. This is a note for writers, not a rule the CSS enforces.

Pl. 04Surfaces & linesStable · grounds, objects, lines, the exception
A page is a sequence of surfaces.

One page tone with occasional cards on it reads as monotonous no matter how good the tone is. A surface context remaps the semantic tokens for everything inside it, so the same markup works on any of them with no overrides. Alternating them down a page is the whole fix.

There are five, and there is no warm one. A band of the emphasis tone turns it into a second page tone, so warm is documented in Pl. 01 as a colour with three uses. That is the difference between a colour and a surface and it is worth holding: a surface is somewhere components live, a colour is something a component is.

A page may have more than one dark section (15 October). There is no dark theme; it was removed on 14 October as too heavy for the brand. A dark band is the strongest emphasis in the system and costs no chroma, which makes it the right tool for a reader who will not be shouted at: the closing call to action, a single claim, the figure the page exists for, the footer. Two dark bands never sit back to back; a light band goes between them.

Tone sets back. The edge makes an object.

The system has one tone besides the page, a wash of ink, and one line, the hairline. They never do each other’s job. A tone says “set back”: a band, the console’s sides, the recess a choice sits in. An edge says “object”: a card, an input, a library entry, and its face is the page tone, so on a ground it is the brightest thing and reads as lifted. Warm is the exception, kept for callouts and violators.

Renamed on 16 October. The tone was called --panel, a name that means a raised board, while the console used it as a background. Both uses mean “quieter than the page”, so the tone keeps one meaning and the names now say which job is being done. Three small objects are filled with it, a badge, an avatar and your own message, because each is meant to be quieter than what is beside it: your question is quieter than the answer. That is the tone’s meaning at object size, not a second meaning, so it does not dilute the language. What would: a card filled with it on the page, or a ground with an edge round it.

--ground

A region set back from the page: a band (.surface--ground), the tray’s recess. Two percent of ink. Was --panel.

--ground-2

Further back: the console’s rail, library and project page (.surface--ground-2). Four percent, the darkest tone text sits on; --ink-55 measures 4.76:1 on it. New.

--face

The face of an object on a ground: a card, a library entry, the open chat in the rail. The page tone. Was --panel-face.

--fill

A small object that is itself set back: a badge, an avatar, your message. The ground’s tone at object size. New.

--edge

An object’s hairline, 1px of --line-2. Was --panel-border; --panel-elev, always none, is gone.

Former names

--panel, --panel-face and --panel-border stay as aliases for production’s console.css only. Nothing here uses them.

Pl. 05TextureStable · the dashed grid, still or rendered
Use it freely. Never two in a row.

The dashed grid can run on as many sections of a page as want it (15 October; it used to be rationed). Two rules hold. It always fades: every textured region takes a mask, so the grid is never a hard-edged rectangle. Two textured sections never touch: put a plain band between them, or the two fades meet and the grid reads as wallpaper with a crease in it. Anything sitting on the grid is opaque.

Put copy on the clear side. .textured--side, the right-hand fade that stays clear under a hero’s copy, is still in the catalog for the hero. .textured--bottom (15 October) holds the grid at the foot and fades it out upward, full width: the console’s chat sits on it, so the grid gathers under the composer and the conversation above reads on clear paper.

The rendered grid.

The same dashed grid, developing cell by cell as its band scrolls in and dissolving as it leaves: scroll-linked, never looped, in ink only. Pl. 06Logo, line & faviconStable
The bracketed dove, and the name beside it.

The logo is one lockup, an image: the dove in its brackets, drawn in the dither, and PEACE INTEL set beside it. It is always the file, never typed, and never in colour. This is version 6 (16 October), from Matt’s Figma masters in Resources/Logos/: the name is two words, as it is in a sentence, and the brackets are heavier.

Sizethe image scales; the rules do not
Faviconin every page’s head, after the title

The logo’s four focus brackets with nothing inside, on an opaque white tile. Each bracket is one line weight (two cells of 32) and sits two cells in from the edge, so at 16px every line and the margin are one pixel. The outer border was removed on 16 October (Matt): the brackets alone carry the tile, and because it is opaque the one drawing works on light and dark tabs alike. The dove was dropped from it the same day: at 16px it was a smudge. Copy the three <link> lines from the head of landing.html.

Never typed

The name in a heading or a sentence is “Peace Intel”, two words. As a logo it is only ever the image; never set PEACE_INTEL in the mono with an underscore, which was an earlier lockup.

Ink or paper, nothing else

Ink on paper, paper on a dark band. Never in the accent, never recoloured, outlined or shadowed, and never over texture or a veil where the grid would cross it.

With partners

Peace Intel leads at its own size. Partner logos sit in an .orgs row (Pl. 12) after a mono label, such as “A project of”, at matched height and in greyscale. Co-branding with Rotary needs Rotary International’s approval.

The brand line.

One line, chosen 16 October from seven, for clarity and trust with an institutional reader. It goes wherever the brand speaks in a few words: under the logo, on the brand board, a report’s cover, a deck’s title slide, a badge. It is a sentence, so it ends with a full stop, and it is set in the serif in sentence case, never in capitals.

Clear evidence for consequential decisions.

Word for word

Never shortened, reworded or split over a logo. Where there is no room for all five words, there is no room for the line.

Under the logo, not beside it

A gutter of the logo’s height below it, left-aligned with the brackets, at --t-h3 or larger, in --ink.

Not a headline

Pages keep their own headlines. Home’s, “Decisions on peace and security are too important to leave to chance.”, stays Home’s, and is not the brand’s line.

On a page, the logo and the line are .brand-sign: the logo’s img, then p.brand-line at --t-h2. Home’s footer opens with it, on the dark logo.

Pl. 07ImageryStable tiers · the veil is an Exploration
One loud thing per page.

Four tiers of image and motion. At most one tier-one element per page, and never two tiers competing in the same viewport. Without the rule a page ends up with a hero video, a section video and a grid texture all fighting, which reads as noise rather than atmosphere.

Tier 1

Hero motion

Dithered loop behind the headline. One per page.

Tier 2

Band texture

Dithered loop behind a section. One per band.

Tier 3

Figure

A still diagram, chart or specimen. Framed, opaque.

Tier 4

Grid vignette

The dashed texture. As often as a page wants, always faded, and never on two sections that touch.

All motion assets share one treatment: medium cell, seven percent mono Bayer dither, greyscale, multiply blend. That treatment is the brand's image language, so a new asset that is not dithered will look like it came from somewhere else. On dark surfaces the blend switches to screen, because multiply would erase the asset entirely.

The dashed grid is fine to use. What is not fine is a hard-edged rectangle of it. Every texture has to fade out, through a vignette mask, a band mask, or the algorithmic falloff the canvas version uses. An unmasked texture reads as a panel that someone forgot to style; the same texture with its edges dissolved reads as atmosphere. That is the whole difference, and it is one line of mask-image. Texture also does not go on top of imagery — two textures in one rectangle read as a screen door, which is why the grid came off the stage in Pl. 05.

The dither was picked in July because it looked good. It turns out to be the product metaphor as well, an image resolving out of noise, which is why the motion language in the next plate is built from it.

The veil.

A subject shot on white, a dove or an object on a seamless, dithered and blended into the page so the ground disappears and the subject floats with no box and no edge. It is a quality of the page rather than an object on it, so it sits behind type, and the copy over it needs no opaque face. Its still form, .vmap--still, maps a photograph of a subject through the same monochrome ramp; Home and Rotary use it for figures. The video map, footage that fills its frame, is retired for now and in the archive.

Pl. 08MotionStable · a closed set
The register without the costume.

No typewriter, no terminal cursor, no redaction bars, no scanlines. Those are set dressing for a spy film. What intelligence work actually feels like is evidence resolving out of noise, a print coming up to density, an instrument pulling into focus, a rule drawn before anything is written under it, and claims arriving in order of confidence. Scroll past them to fire; press replay to see them again.

One entry move, decided 15 October. Claims arrive in order of confidence: each child of a group is laid down in turn under its own soft mask, with a six-pixel rise. It goes on the container and its children are what move: the parts of a lockup, the rows of a list, the cards of a grid. It runs 900ms with 126ms between children, slow enough to watch, because a reveal that finishes before the eye arrives did not happen. Resolve, develop, focus and rule are in the archive.

.mo-dealon a card · the children are dealt
Dealt

Laid down, in order.

Every child carries its own soft mask, staggered, with a six-pixel rise under it. The mask edge is 12% of the child’s own height, tight enough that each item has a definite top, so the block reads as being placed rather than as being uncovered.

.mo-dealon a list · strongest evidence first

Also here, though it is an affordance rather than a reveal: hovering a citation ●1 draws the hairline connection to its source instead of just tinting. It answers a pointer, so it is instant.

Every one of these is killed outright by prefers-reduced-motion, and every hidden starting state is gated behind [data-js]. With script off the content is simply there. Animation is never load-bearing for legibility, which is the one rule in this plate that is not a taste call.

Pl. 15Don’tMeasured, not hypothetical
Every one of these actually happened.

These are failure modes found in this codebase, not cautionary inventions.

Don't invent type sizes

index.html uses 19 whole-pixel sizes. rotary-a.html grew to 25 including 9.5 and 14.5px; problems.html to 32.

Don't write off-grid spacing

24 distinct vertical margin values in one page. Every space is a --sp-* step.

Don't hardcode white on dark

rgba(255,255,255,.6) and #fff across .ctaband, .close and .card. Use a surface context.

Don't fork the palette per page

Production sets brown at the base and two page files override to orange, so the live site renders two brands by route.

Don't wallpaper the grid

It was under the entire page, unmasked. Use it on as many sections as you like, faded, and never on two that touch.

Don't put a translucent card on texture

The dashes read through the face as dirt. Every surface in this system is opaque.

Don't re-declare a shared rule to change it

All three section{padding} values came from re-declaring the rule after the block. Edit the block.

Don't give one class two meanings

.chip was a solid pill in four files and bare text in two. .cta was a button in one lineage and a section in another.

Don't use the accent decoratively

It marks evidence, primary action, focus and active state. More than three or four accented spots means it is over-accented.

Don't reach for the typewriter

Terminal cursors, redaction bars and scanlines are costume. The motion language is resolution, not performance.

Don't make a band of the warm tone

Warm is a colour with three uses — callout, violator, field ramp. A band of it turns the emphasis tone into a second page tone.

Don't put white on the accent

Brand orange measures 4.29:1 under white and 4.33:1 under ink. Anything filled with the accent writes --accent-fill and --on-accent together, never --accent with a literal white.

Don't tint what cannot be clicked

The evidence badges carried the button's tint and read as buttons. The tint means act; a label that only labels is ink.

Don't time a reveal like a control

320ms is right for a hover and invisible for an entry. Anything that reveals runs 900 to 1600; if all you can say afterwards is "something appeared", it is too fast.

Don't put chroma in a background field

A field is the largest object on its page, so its colour becomes the page's colour. The ramp is paper and ink.

Who it is for

Two kinds of decision.

The image and its tab. The clip fills the margin, 208px wide against the icon’s 50 (the column widens for it), as a veil: grayscale and multiplied, so its white ground drops into the page, and inverted on a dark band. A 2px tab sits on the section’s hairline over the margin only, so the edge thickens where the mark is. The label moves under it, and the heading stands alone.

Who it is for

Two kinds of decision.

Folded. In a narrow document the margin becomes a row above the heading, the clip drops to 64px and the tab shrinks to the width of the mark and its name. It folds on the width of the document, not the screen, so a document in a narrow console column folds the way a page does on a phone.

The image or the icon

The clips are the brand’s most recognisable thing and give the margin presence the icons could not; the icons name each section’s subject, which two clips cannot. Moving images suit a long page with few sections; icons suit a page whose sections each need a name.

One name in every place

The margin’s short name is the section’s only label: the eyebrow above the heading moves under the mark. With the strip gone it is also the only table of contents, so keep the names short and literal.

Ink and hairline only

The tab is ink and the clips are veils, so the device sits on paper, a ground, a dark band or beside the texture without a variant. Nothing in it moves except the clip and the scroll.

Where it goes

Long pages such as the landing page and the Rotary pitch. A document in the console keeps a numeral in the same margin, because its sections come from an answer and are cited by number. Not the chat: an answer’s parts stay numbered serif headings.

Pl. 18Documentary imagesDecided 16 Oct · 18 · A1
The places the evidence is about.

Every picture in the system so far is an object on a white seamless: a dove, a glass, an olive branch. The corpus is about real places, and an institution paying for it will expect to see one. One way to carry a documentary photograph in the brand’s own treatment, both on the same picture of a street in Mosul’s Old City after the war with the Islamic State, chosen because it shows a place and its damage and no one in it. It replaced a view across the Tigris on 16 October: its sky and water were one flat tone each, and the dither had nothing to work on; rubble is detail from edge to edge.

Places, not suffering

Show the place and what happened to it: streets, buildings, a river, a market reopened. Never casualties, never a person in distress, and no identifiable face without consent. A brand sold to institutions earns trust by restraint.

Always named and dated

A documentary picture is evidence, so it carries what evidence carries: where, when, and who took it, in the caption under it. Open-licence pictures are credited even when the licence does not ask.

One per page, as a figure

Tier three: framed, square, beside copy and never behind it. On a page that has the hero veil, a documentary picture is the second and last image tier on screen.

Why it floats

A bar with a margin is an object over the page, so it obeys the object rule: an edge round the page tone. A full-width bar is part of the page and has only a divider under it, as .nav does today.

Why it compacts

On a long page the bar is read once and then only needed to get home or to act. Pulled in to the logo, it stops covering the column, which matters most on a phone, where a full bar is a sixth of the screen.

What it costs

A small script that watches the scroll direction and opens the bar when the dove is tapped. On a page that snaps (Home) it compacts on the first screen change.
