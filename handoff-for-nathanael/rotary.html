Written the way Rotary reads

We read Rotary’s rules so your club doesn’t have to.

Rotary publishes exactly what a peace grant has to contain: the project types it will fund, the five elements every application must include, and the measures it accepts. Peace Intel has those criteria built in, next to the peacebuilding evidence base, so your application is checked as you write it.

How it works

From idea to application.

Four steps. Start wherever your club actually is: with a project in mind, or with nothing but the wish to do something useful.

01

Pick your intervention area

Tell us the country and what your club cares about. Peace Intel pulls the conflict context and matches it to the project types Rotary will fund under peace and conflict resolution, so you start inside the rules.

02

Connect your approach to real evidence

For every part of your project we find what has been tried in comparable places and what happened. We say plainly whether the evidence is strong, mixed or untested, and name the one or two sources that carry the weight.

03

Refine with best practices

We check the project against Rotary’s own requirements: the community assessment, the training plan, the trainers, the sustainability plan, the monitoring plan. Anything missing gets named, with one line on what would fix it.

04

Synthesise into a complete application

Every field drafted or annotated in your own voice, with a one-page rationale your club can read and a monitoring plan built on a measure Rotary already recognises.
