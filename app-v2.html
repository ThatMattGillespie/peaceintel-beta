Is this proposal's theory of change supported by evidence? It assumes community dialogue alone will reduce intercommunal violence in Darfur.
Peace Intel

The proposal's core assumption — that dialogue alone reduces intercommunal violence — has mixed support. In comparable cases, dialogue-only interventions showed limited durable effect on violence levels, while interventions that combined dialogue with a credible security component performed substantially better. The strongest evidence concerns peacekeeping presence: deployments of several thousand troops and several hundred police dramatically reduced civilian killings across African civil wars, 1991–2008. [synthesis]

On Darfur specifically, the case record shows intercommunal dialogue processes during UNAMID's deployment repeatedly stalled when not backed by protection guarantees; localized agreements in 2009–2010 broke down within months of force drawdowns. [case fact]

One caveat: the strongest counter-example in the corpus, an evaluation of community dialogue in eastern DRC, exists in the library but its full text hasn't been cleanly extracted yet — I can point you to the original PDF.

Bottom line: the evidence supports dialogue as a component, not a standalone mechanism. You may want to ask the applicant how the program handles security guarantees.
